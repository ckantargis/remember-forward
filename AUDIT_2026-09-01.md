## FINDING 1 — TIER E WAS BUILT ON A FALSE CITATION

Published: *Tier E · "Lead-Lined Granite" · $500–$1,500 · **100,000+ years** ·
"Nuclear industry permanence standard."*

The March-2026 justification: *"Lead-lined granite is essentially permanent on
geological timescales and is what the nuclear industry actually uses for
archive-grade storage."*

**False.** It fuses two true facts into an untrue one:

- Granite **is** a real repository host rock (Onkalo, Forsmark). True.
- Lead **is** used in the nuclear industry — for **radiation shielding in
  transport casks**. True, and irrelevant to containment.
- "Lead-lined granite as an archive standard" is **not a thing.** The actual
  design is **KBS-3: copper canister over a cast-iron insert, in a bentonite
  clay buffer.**

**"100,000+ years" is also unsupportable.** Repositories claim that figure for
the *geology*, never for the container, and only behind a formal safety case.

Lead's own problems, independent of the citation: creeps under sustained load so
it cannot be structural; toxic, a real regulatory burden for an institutional
install; forms a galvanic couple with titanium in moisture, becoming the
sacrificial anode and being consumed. **And `containers/ERRATA.md` already ranks
`welded metal > cast lead` — lead is not the best seal by the project's own
analysis.**

### REPLACEMENT — Tier E · Granite Vault

| | |
|---|---|
| Body | Carved granite or local massive stone |
| Buffer | **Bentonite clay backfill** — swells on wetting to self-seal, buffers chemistry, cheap, non-toxic. The actual engineered barrier in real repositories, and never once mentioned in this project before today. |
| Containment | **Welded titanium inner vessel** — no gasket, no elastomer, nothing to fail. Optionally borosilicate glass within it. |
| Seal | Welded. Cast lead demoted to optional redundant secondary at the stone joint only. |
| Jointing | Geopolymer — low-temperature cure from local clay, reproducible by a future civilisation |
| Lifespan | **10,000+ years** — defensible for welded Ti in bentonite. Not 100,000; that number requires a safety case we do not have. |

## FINDING 2 — OTHER TIER CLAIMS THAT DO NOT HOLD

Every one of these is currently published.

**Tier A · Schedule 80 PVC · "500 – 1,000 years."** `CLAUDE.md` already flags
that *PVC may be archivally disqualified (HCl outgassing)* — the degradation is
autocatalytic and the released acid attacks the contents. PVC has existed since
the 1930s; **there is no empirical basis for a 500-year figure.** This is the
cheapest tier and the one aimed at "maximum household distribution", so it is
the most-sold and the least-supported claim on the site.

**Tier B · Titanium Sphere.** Three problems:
- *"Viton O-rings"* — **directly contradicts the project's own errata**, which
  rated elastomer last and concluded "weld it if it must outlast 100–300 years."
  The correction was made to `container_and_tiering_v2` and never to the site.
- *"Grade 5 Ti-6Al-4V"* — Grade 5 is the strength alloy. **Grade 2 CP titanium
  has better corrosion resistance for burial**, and Grade 2 is what the project
  decided for plates. Inconsistent.
- *"Aerogel insulation"* — serves no archival function in a buried vessel.
- *"Pyramid cavity at Giza angle"* — **no engineering basis.** This is
  pseudo-archaeology in a product spec, and it is the single most
  reputationally dangerous line on the site given the stated goal of partnering
  with Long Now, AWA and MOM. Recommend deletion.

**Tier C · Ocean Capsule.** Two problems:
- *"HDPE hull + zinc sacrificial anodes"* — **zinc anodes cannot protect a
  plastic hull.** Galvanic protection requires metal in electrical continuity
  with the anode. As written this is a technical error.
- *"Unreachable, unjurisdictable"* — **legally false and a liability.** The high
  seas are governed by UNCLOS, and deliberate deposition of objects on the
  seabed is regulated by the London Convention and Protocol; it may constitute
  dumping. Advertising "unjurisdictable" as a feature invites exactly the
  problem the business-conduct policy exists to avoid.
- `ERRATA.md` separately notes `gd_11`'s self-righting claim has no calculation
  behind it. Still true.

**Tier D · Hastelloy C-276 · "Outperforms titanium in corrosive zones."**
Overstated, not flatly false. Hastelloy wins in *reducing* acids; **titanium
wins in oxidising chlorides.** Needs the qualifier. *"Nuclear-grade chemical
resistance"* is marketing with no referent.

**Tier F · Mountain Archive · "Geological timescale."** Unsupportable as
written. The cave may last that long; the contents will not.

**Cross-cutting: every lifespan figure on the site is unsourced.** They are the
same class of claim that failed on Tier E. Either source them or reframe them
as design intent rather than performance.
