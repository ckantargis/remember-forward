**Subject:** Technical questions from an independent plate-engraving preservation project

Dear Rosetta Project team,

I'm an independent researcher building a physical multilingual preservation project — a series of engraved metal plates containing language documentation across 50+ languages, intended for long-term archival deposit. I've reached a point where your organization's published methodology would save me significant redundant work, and I'm writing to ask a few specific technical questions.

**Parallel text:** For each language's running-text plate, I want to include a short standardized passage that exists in verified human translations across a very broad range of languages, to give a future reader a triangulation anchor. I'm considering Article 1 of the UDHR (OHCHR, 500+ languages). Do you have experience with this approach, or a different parallel-text corpus you've found more reliable for script diversity and translation quality?

**Interlinear glossing:** I intend to add word-by-word glossing below each parallel passage following the Leipzig Glossing Rules. Are there open resources you're aware of that apply this consistently across a wide range of language families?

**Language metadata:** What fields do you record alongside each language entry to help an uninstructed future reader identify the language — script family, geographic region, time period, living/extinct status?

**Engraving specifications:** We're targeting laser-annealed 316L stainless steel at roughly 12×17cm plates. Have you documented minimum feature sizes or stroke weights for long-term legibility at this scale?

Any guidance on any of these would be genuinely useful. I'm happy to share methodology notes or early plate designs if a reciprocal exchange would be worthwhile.

Thank you for the work the Rosetta Project has done — it is directly relevant to what I'm attempting.
